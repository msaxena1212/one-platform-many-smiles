# Real Estate Property Management System
## End-to-End Finance Management & Accounting Architecture Specification

**Document Version:** 1.0  
**Purpose:** Definitive functional, accounting, technical, control, integration, and reporting specification for the Finance Management module of a Real Estate Property Management System.

---

# 1. Document Objective

This document defines the complete Finance Management architecture for a Real Estate Property Management System.

The Finance module must not operate as a collection of independent screens. It must operate as a centralized accounting engine where every financial event generated by:

- Leasing
- Tenant Management
- Property Management
- Procurement
- Purchase Orders
- GRN
- Vendor Management
- Maintenance
- Contracts
- Payments
- Receipts
- PDCs
- Security Deposits
- Refunds
- Payroll
- Assets
- Banking
- Cash Management

is converted into controlled, auditable, double-entry accounting transactions.

The fundamental architecture is:

```text
Operational Business Event
        ↓
Financial Event
        ↓
Accounting Rule Engine
        ↓
Validation & Controls
        ↓
Journal Entry
        ↓
General Ledger
        ↓
Sub-Ledgers
        ↓
Trial Balance
        ↓
Financial Statements
        ↓
Management Reporting
```

The Finance Engine must ensure:

> **Every financial number has a source, every source has an accounting rule, every posted entry is balanced, every posting is auditable, and every financial report can be traced back to the underlying transaction.**

---

# 2. Core Finance Architecture Principles

The following principles are mandatory.

## 2.1 General Ledger Is the Financial Source of Truth

The General Ledger is the authoritative financial record.

Reports must ultimately derive from posted accounting entries.

```text
Source Transaction
        ↓
Posted Journal
        ↓
GL
        ↓
Financial Reports
```

Operational tables must not independently calculate official financial statements.

---

# 3. Real-Time Synchronization

All official financial reports must update in real time **after a transaction is successfully posted**.

"Real-time" does not mean that a draft transaction immediately changes the financial statements.

## 3.1 Transaction States

Every financial transaction should have:

```text
DRAFT
    ↓
SUBMITTED
    ↓
APPROVED
    ↓
POSTED
    ↓
REVERSED / CLOSED
```

Possible exception states:

```text
ACCOUNTING_PENDING
ACCOUNTING_FAILED
CANCELLED
REJECTED
```

---

# 4. Draft vs Posted Accounting

## Draft

Draft transactions must not affect:

- General Ledger
- Trial Balance
- P&L
- Balance Sheet
- Cash Flow Statement
- Official Cash Book
- Official Bank Book

They may appear in operational and approval dashboards.

## Approved

An approved transaction is authorized but should only affect official accounting once posted.

## Posted

A posted transaction affects:

- GL
- Sub-ledger
- Trial Balance
- P&L
- Balance Sheet
- Cash Flow
- Relevant operational reports

## Cancelled

Draft transactions may be cancelled.

Posted transactions must never simply be deleted.

---

# 5. Posted Transaction Immutability

Once posted:

- Do not edit
- Do not delete
- Do not overwrite

If a correction is required:

```text
Original Entry
      ↓
Reversal Entry
      ↓
Corrected Entry
```

This ensures complete auditability.

# 16. Journal Validation

Every posted journal must satisfy:

```text
Total Debit = Total Credit
```

Additionally:

```text
Debit >= 0
Credit >= 0
```

and a single journal line must not contain both debit and credit.

A journal should contain at least two lines.

# 20. PDC State Machine

The PDC lifecycle must be controlled.

```text
RECEIVED
    ↓
IN_HAND
    ↓
DEPOSITED
    ↓
BANK_CLEARING
    ↓
CLEARED
```

Alternative states:

```text
IN_HAND
 ├── RETURNED
 └── CANCELLED

BANK_CLEARING
 └── BOUNCED

BOUNCED
 └── RE-PRESENTED
```

A PDC cannot arbitrarily jump between states.

---

# 21. PDC Collected for Rent

Assume:

```text
Rent Invoice = ₹100,000
PDC = ₹100,000
```

If the invoice is already posted:

```text
DR 12900 - PDC In Hand                 ₹100,000
    CR 12413 - Tenant Receivables          ₹100,000
```

The PDC settles the receivable.

It does not create revenue.

---

# 22. Rent Revenue Recognition

The rent invoice creates the revenue:

```text
DR 12413 - Tenant Receivables          ₹100,000
    CR 41100 - Rental Revenue              ₹100,000
```

Applicable tax lines should also be generated through the tax engine.

Therefore:

```text
Invoice
→ Revenue Recognition

PDC
→ Settlement Instrument
```

These are separate accounting concepts.

---

# 23. PDC Collected Before Invoice

If the tenant provides a PDC before the corresponding rent invoice exists:

```text
DR 12900 - PDC In Hand
    CR 21400 - Customer PDC Liability
```

When the rent invoice is generated:

```text
DR 12413 - Tenant Receivables
    CR 41100 - Rental Revenue
```

When the PDC is allocated:

```text
DR 21400 - Customer PDC Liability
    CR 12413 - Tenant Receivables
```

This clears the temporary PDC liability against the receivable.

---

# 24. PDC for Security Deposit

If PDC represents a security deposit:

```text
DR 12900 - PDC In Hand
    CR 21500 - Security Deposit Liability
```

No revenue is recognized.

---

# 25. PDC Deposit into Bank

A robust banking architecture should use:

```text
12010 - Bank Clearing / Cheques Under Collection
```

When deposited:

```text
DR 12010 - Bank Clearing
    CR 12900 - PDC In Hand
```

This means:

> The cheque has been deposited but has not yet become cleared bank cash.

---

# 26. PDC Clearance

When the bank confirms clearance:

```text
DR 12000 - Bank Operating Account
    CR 12010 - Bank Clearing
```

Do not credit rental revenue at clearance.

Revenue was already recognized through the invoice.

---

# 27. PDC Returned Before Deposit

If a PDC is still in hand and returned to the tenant:

For rent:

```text
DR 12413 - Tenant Receivables
    CR 12900 - PDC In Hand
```

For security deposit:

```text
DR 21500 - Security Deposit Liability
    CR 12900 - PDC In Hand
```

The original accounting relationship must be reversed.

---

# 28. PDC Cancelled

PDC cancellation is allowed only when:

```text
PDC Status = IN_HAND
```

or another explicitly permitted pre-deposit state.

The cancellation must reverse the original accounting.

A cleared PDC cannot simply be cancelled.

---

# 29. Rent Paid in Cash Instead of PDC

This is a mandatory business scenario.

Example:

```text
Rent = ₹50,000
PDC = ₹50,000
```

PDC is currently in hand.

Tenant chooses cash.

## Step 1 — Cancel PDC

```text
DR 12413 - Tenant Receivables
    CR 12900 - PDC In Hand
```

## Step 2 — Record Cash Payment

```text
DR 10100 - Cash In Hand
    CR 12413 - Tenant Receivables
```

Final result:

```text
PDC = Cancelled
Cash = +₹50,000
Tenant AR = ₹0
Revenue = Unchanged
```

---

# 30. Cash + PDC Combination

```text

```

```text

```

# 37. PDC Cleared Then Bank Reverses

If a cleared cheque is later reversed by the bank:

The system must create a controlled reversal.

It must:

1. Reverse the bank settlement.
2. Reinstate the underlying receivable/liability where appropriate.
3. Record bank charges.
4. Preserve the original clearance event.
5. Never delete the original clearance transaction.

# 134. Critical Edge Cases

The system must explicitly handle the following.

## Duplicate Payment

Same payment received twice.

Expected:

```text
One accounting event
Second event rejected/idempotently handled
```

## Payment Greater Than Invoice

Remaining amount becomes:

```text
Advance
or
Unallocated Receipt
or
Refund
```

## Invoice Cancelled After Payment

Payment becomes:

```text
Advance
Unallocated
or Refundable
```

according to business rules.

## PDC Bounced

Receivable reinstated.

## PDC Returned Before Deposit

Original PDC accounting reversed.

## PDC Cancelled Because Rent Paid in Cash

PDC reversed first, cash receipt posted second.

## PDC Already Cleared

Cannot be cancelled.

Additional cash payment becomes overpayment/advance.

## Security Deposit Partially Refunded

Remaining liability tracked.

## Security Deposit Adjusted Against Rent

Deposit liability transferred to tenant receivable.

## Tenant Moves Units

Historical transactions retain original unit.

Future transactions use new unit.

## Property Sold

Historical transactions remain intact.

Asset disposal is separately recorded.

## Vendor Bank Changed

Approval and audit required.

## Closed Period Transaction

Rejected or controlled adjustment.

## Duplicate Webhook

Idempotent processing.

## Bank Reversal

Controlled reversal.

## Partial Bank Reconciliation

Partial matching supported.

## Tax Rule Changed

Historical transactions retain previous tax rule.

## Rent Rate Changed

Future schedule updated; historical invoices unchanged.

## Vendor Invoice Across Multiple Properties

Dimension allocation.

## Negative Cash

Hard stop or controlled exception.

# 145. Final Finance System Principle

The Finance Management System should be designed around five immutable principles:

### 1. Event-Driven

Every business event creates a financial event.

### 2. Double-Entry

Every posted transaction must balance.

### 3. Purpose-Aware

The same payment instrument can have different accounting treatment depending on whether it represents:

```text
Rent
Security Deposit
Advance
Other Receivable
```

### 4. Fully Auditable

Nothing posted disappears.

### 5. Reconciliation-First

Every subledger must reconcile with its corresponding control account.

# 146. Final End-to-End Accounting Philosophy

The Finance Engine should ultimately answer all of these questions:

```text
Where did this amount originate?
Who created it?
Who approved it?
What property does it belong to?
What tenant/vendor/owner does it belong to?
What contract does it relate to?
What accounting rule generated it?
Which journal posted it?
Which GL account received it?
Which subledger does it affect?
Was it subsequently reversed?
Was it reconciled?
Did it affect cash?
Did it affect revenue?
Did it affect a liability?
What is the current outstanding balance?
```
